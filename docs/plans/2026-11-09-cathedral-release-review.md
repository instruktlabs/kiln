## Owner follow-up: balanced interior, sculpture definition and gold

This follow-up supersedes the candlelight candidate below. The owner found its altar too bright, rear spaces too dark, sculpture too flat, and the crucifix halo black. The interior fill now follows the nave, aisles, transept arms, faceted apse and seven separate rear chapels. Its boundary passes through the stone thickness so inward wall faces receive light without leaving a dark floor band. A normal-aware diffuse lobe and height falloff retain surface direction and vault depth. The exterior sun, hemisphere and exposure are unchanged. Candle intensity is reduced to 2.2 per higher-tier group or 3.5 for the single lower-tier group.

Interior WebGPU SSAO adds local indirect-light occlusion at a 0.3 m radius and half resolution, with eight Economy samples and sixteen on Balanced/High. Minimal and WebGL use the improved lighting without this pass. The opaque depth/normal prepass includes only the cathedral and furnishings; transparent glass and direct stained sunlight are not multiplied by AO. Outside the interior, the original single render path is used. An independent prepass camera prevents Three's scene/camera render-list collision, reproduced during the first active browser test and covered by a regression test. Resources and borrowed object layer bits are restored on teardown; failure paths retain direct rendering.

The saved Gold_Leaf and Gilding materials were already metallic gold. The scene's absent reflection environment caused their black appearance. A once-per-world/light, 256-pixel PMREM capture of the actual sanctuary now supplies reflections to cloned interior gold materials only, at intensity 2.2. It hides reflecting meshes during capture and restores visibility afterward. It neither recolors nor makes the gold emissive, and does not set a global environment. A new Suspended crucifix viewpoint makes its halo and inscription inspectable.

## Earlier owner follow-up: candlelight and scene organization

The owner requested a brighter altar preview and interior, with candles and flame effects, while retaining the first exterior picture. The scene now lights its six existing candles with depth-tested tapered flame cores, optional small halos and deterministic flicker bounded to 4.5%. Reduced motion holds the flames and light intensity steady. Two groups of candlelight use cached opaque point-light shadows at 512 pixels per cube face; the lower tiers use one 256-pixel cube. Direct candle illumination is omitted if shadows are unavailable. The flame geometry adds 1,200 triangles. The pillar-occlusion fixture verifies an actual choir pier intercepts a ray within the candle light's range. The stained-sun projection is unchanged.

A spatially limited warm-neutral diffuse fill makes the nave and sanctuary more readable without changing exterior exposure, sun or hemisphere settings. This is an artistic indirect-bounce approximation, not simulated global illumination. The candle lights supply the localized warmth and actual opaque shadows. Their final intensity was reduced after visual review to retain detail in the carved retable.
